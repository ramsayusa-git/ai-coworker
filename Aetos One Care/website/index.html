How it works

Four steps, one continuous record.

No file exports, no re-typing vitals, no "please send me your last report".

Capture

The patient app pairs over BLE with the ECG2 chest sensor, BP cuff, pulse oximeter or glucometer. Readings are signed on-device and timestamped.

Stream

Waveforms and vitals ride a WebRTC data channel into the same room as the video. Sub-second, with local buffering when the line drops.

Decide

The doctor console renders Lead II beside the patient history. The scribe drafts the note; CDS flags interactions. The doctor signs.

Persist

Consult, waveform and prescription land in one FHIR R4 record, linked to ABHA under a consent artefact the patient can revoke.

Built for the 2026 rulebook

Compliance designed in, not retrofitted.

CDSCO issued final Medical Device Software guidance in July 2026 and it classifies by function, not by disclaimer. DPDPA obligations phase in on a fixed calendar. We designed the consent ledger, the audit trail and the AI change-control plan before the first clinical feature shipped.

Questions

The things buyers actually ask.

Do you replace our existing EMR?

Not necessarily. The device and consult layer can run alongside an incumbent EMR over FHIR R4 and HL7 v2.5. Hospitals running Bahmni or a KareXpert-class HIMS usually keep it and add the signal layer.

Which devices are supported?

The ikinloop ECG2 chest sensor, standard BLE blood-pressure cuffs, pulse oximeters and glucometers, with a device registry that carries calibration state and signal-quality scoring per reading.

Is the AI making diagnoses?

No. Scribe and decision support are assistive, scoped to screening, and every output is attributed to and signed by a registered practitioner. Rhythm classification stays behind a feature flag pending clearance.

Can patients take their records elsewhere?

Yes — that is the point of ABHA linking and six-format export. A record you cannot leave with is a record you do not own.

What does it cost?

Published, per-clinic, on the pricing page. Almost nobody in this market publishes pricing; we think that vacuum is a trust problem, not a strategy.
